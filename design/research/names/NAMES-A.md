# TV summons: name clips (set A)

Names: Aaron, Harry, Aidan, Sol, Joshua, Jamie, Kyle, James, Beth.
(Set B, covering Emma, Matthew, Thomas, Munro, Jenkins, Luke, Jimmy and Eva, is with another agent.)

This is research only. Nothing was downloaded or ripped. Links are to findable public clips. Timestamps
marked "~" are approximate and need checking by ear before anyone cuts a clip. Assume every clip is
**copyrighted** unless it is flagged otherwise. Playing them at a private house party is the same
risk as playing YouTube on the telly, but they must not be bundled into anything we distribute.

Notes on the method:
- getyarn.io (search by line, e.g. `getyarn.io/yarn-find?text=kyle`) is the fastest way to find
  the exact second of a line in films and TV. It blocked automated fetches, so check it by hand.
- Soundboard sites (myinstants, voicy, soundboardguy) already host many of these as 1–3 s cuts,
  but their uploads are not licensed either.

## Aaron

1. **"A-A-RON!": Key & Peele, "Substitute Teacher" (Comedy Central, 2012). TV sketch.**
   Mr. Garvey (Keegan-Michael Key) mangles the register, then explodes: "A-A-Ron!". It is
   shouted, and he says it several times with rising rage. Clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dd7FixvoKBw
   (the full sketch; the A-A-Ron beat is in the first ~40 s). There is also an isolated cut at
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A0VGhDJs2qc. The usable bit is ~1–1.5 s.
   *Why it's funny:* the sketch has 230M+ views, and it is THE "Aaron" meme. Everyone does the voice.
   It is perfect for summoning: the whole joke is a name being called out wrong and angrily.
2. **"Aaron Burr, sir": Hamilton (Original Broadway Cast, 2015). Musical.**
   Hamilton sings "Pardon me, are you Aaron Burr, sir?" at the start of the song "Aaron Burr, Sir".
   It is sung clearly. Clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BUKI0OsTym4 (official lyric video,
   ~0:00–0:05). The usable bit is ~1.5 s for "Aaron Burr, sir". It is less funny but very recognisable.
3. Fallback: a TTS clip of "Aaron" spoken in a Garvey-style angry teacher read.

## Harry

1. **"HARRY! DID YOU PUT YOUR NAME IN THE GOBLET OF FIRE?!": Harry Potter and the Goblet
   of Fire (2005). Film.** Dumbledore (Michael Gambon) storms in and shakes Harry. The name is
   SHOUTED at the start of the line. Clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fq7WKfyz-Qc. It sits
   at ~1h00m in the film. The usable bit is "HARRY!" alone (~0.6 s), or the full line (~3 s).
   *Why it's funny:* this is a huge meme (book Dumbledore "asked calmly"). The angry full line is a
   brilliant summons, as if the player is in trouble.
2. **"Yer a wizard, Harry": Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (2001). Film.** Hagrid
   (Robbie Coltrane) says it, with the name at the end. Clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3oqtoIu7NvI
   (Movie CLIP #8). The usable bit is ~1.5 s. It is said rather than shouted, so it is a bit quieter.
   *Why it's funny:* it is the most-quoted line in the series.
3. Alternative: the "in 39 languages" compilation (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e6JWU-9_K58)
   could give a random dub each time. That would be a fun variety pick.

## Aidan

There is nothing truly iconic for this name. The best options:
1. **"Aidan!": The Ring (2002) / The Ring Two (2005). Films.** Rachel (Naomi Watts) calls and
   yells for her son Aidan repeatedly. In The Ring Two, she yells "Aidan!" in the bathtub scene
   and he does not answer. The name is SHOUTED alone, which is ideal acoustically. I found no clean
   YouTube clip; use getyarn.io "aidan" to locate it. It would be ~0.8 s.
   *Why it works:* there's a horror-film panic energy, and the name is clean on its own. Not
   many people will place it, though.
2. **Sex and the City (HBO, 1998–2004) / And Just Like That (2021–25): Carrie and Aidan.**
   "Aidan" is said many times, but softly. It's recognisable to SATC fans, not to most lads.
3. **Recommended fallback:** a TTS or friend-recorded "AIDAN!" with a horror sting, or a
   *Rocky* (1976) parody: "AIDAAAN!" read over Rocky's "ADRIAN!" bell and crowd. "Adrian" ≈ "Aidan"
   reads as a deliberate joke for a UK crowd.

## Sol

There is no strong "Sol" clip, but the homophones are excellent:
1. **"Better Call Saul!": Breaking Bad S2E8 "Better Call Saul" (AMC, 2009) and Saul's TV ads.
   TV.** Saul Goodman (Bob Odenkirk) says it in his ads and at the end of his first scene with
   Walt. It is said or barked as a slogan. Clips: compilation
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5u0MR8Kp3BM ("Every 'Better Call Saul'"); ads
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HNdWcP_0ESk. It is ~1.2 s. The soundboard versions already exist
   (https://www.myinstants.com/en/search/?name=better+call+saul).
   *Why it's funny:* it is a massive meme, and "Better call Sol" is a perfect summons line.
2. **Sol Campbell, football. UK commentary.** He headed in the 2006 Champions League final for Arsenal
   (37'). Commentary of the goal: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_D1WPaKzcl8. Commentators shout
   "SOL CAMPBELL!" (~1 s). *Why it works:* it is pure UK football nostalgia. Check whether the
   commentary in that upload actually shouts the name.
3. **"Soul Man": The Blues Brothers (1980) / Sam & Dave (1967). Song.** Only use it if the player
   says "Sol" like "soul". "I'm a SOUL MAN" is a big sung chorus hit (~1.5 s).

## Joshua

1. **Ring announcer "...ANTHONY JOSHUAAA!": Joshua v Klitschko, Wembley, 29 Apr 2017.
   Sport (UK).** Michael Buffer's intro to 90,000 people is a long, roared name. Clips:
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NUqNQXCq3NE (ring walk),
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zMJgAzfIww0 (fight). The usable bit is "JOSH-UAAA!" (~2 s). It is
   SHOUTED. *Why it works:* it is the biggest British boxing night in years, and a hype entrance is
   perfect for being summoned to the TV.
2. **"Joshua": WarGames (1983). Film.** "Joshua" is the backdoor password and the AI's name.
   Falken says "Hello, Joshua" and the computer voice says "Greetings, Professor Falken". It is said
   calmly, and the name is clear (~0.8 s). Find it on getyarn.io with "joshua". This is a nerd-cult
   pick that fewer UK drunks will know.
3. **FREE / public-domain option: "Joshua Fit the Battle of Jericho" (traditional spiritual).**
   It is sung, with "JOSHUA fit the battle of Jericho" opening the chorus (~1.5 s for "Joshua").
   - Paul Robeson & Lawrence Brown 78s on the Internet Archive:
     https://archive.org/details/78_joshua-fit-the-battle-of-jericho_paul-robeson-lawrence-brown_gbia0120617b
     and the 1940 Delta Rhythm Boys version at
     https://archive.org/details/78_joshua-fit-de-battle-of-jericho_delta-rhythm-boys_gbia0049854b.
     The song is public domain. **The recordings need checking.** In the UK, a sound recording is protected
     for 70 years from release, so pre-1955 releases are generally out of copyright here. In the US,
     under the Music Modernization Act timetable, recordings published 1923–1946 stay protected for
     100 years. So as of 2026 only pre-1926 releases are public domain there, and the 1926 Robeson
     record follows in 2027. The 1940/1945 records are not. The Great 78 Project items have no clean licence.
   - Roger McGuinn's Folk Den version on Free Music Archive:
     https://freemusicarchive.org/music/Roger_McGuinn/Folk_Den_Project/20090517161535454/ is
     **CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 US**. Non-commercial use is fine with credit, but no derivatives, so trimming
     it to a 1.5 s cut is arguably a derivative. It is not truly free.
   - Wikimedia Commons has a category with MIDI and sheet music only
     (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Joshua_Fit_the_Battle_of_Jericho). We could render
     the free MIDI ourselves, but it would be instrumental, with no sung name.

## Jamie

1. **"Jamie, pull that up": The Joe Rogan Experience (podcast, recurring). Meme.** Rogan
   asks producer Jamie Vernon to "pull that up". It is said, punchy, with the name first (~1.2 s).
   There are many compilations (search "Jamie pull that up compilation"). KYM:
   https://knowyourmeme.com/photos/1529849-joe-rogan. *Why it's funny:* it is a universally known
   meme and works as a literal summons ("Jamie, get up here").
2. **"Jamie Vardy's having a party": football chant, to "Freed from Desire" (Gala). UK.**
   It started with Leicester fans in 2014 and went national at Euro 2016. It is SUNG by a whole
   crowd, and the full lyric is "...bring your vodka and your charlie" (adults only, which suits a
   drinking game). A charity single version exists:
   https://www.leicestercity.news/news/jamie-vardy-once-had-an-england-chant-that-was-turned-into-a-charity-single/.
   The usable bit is "JAMIE VARDY'S HAVIN' A PARTY" (~2.5 s). This is the best pick for a drunk UK
   crowd; everyone will sing along.
3. **"Jamie's Cryin'": Van Halen (1978). Song.** The chorus is "Oh-oh-oh, Jamie's cryin'"
   (sung). Official video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOjm4I7LRgY. The chorus is at ~0:50 (~1.5 s).

## Kyle

1. **Cartman: "KYYYYYYLLLLLLEEE!": South Park. TV.** It is the long, furious shout,
   and the name is completely isolated. Official clip page:
   https://southpark.cc.com/video-clips/r61n75/south-america-kyyyyyyylllllleee (the clip title says
   "South America". The episode attribution came from a search summary and is not confirmed, so
   check it). There is also a fan compilation "KYLE! KYLE! KYLE KYLE KYLE!":
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8VMbSJ2pm2g. The usable bit is ~1.5–2 s.
   *Why it works:* Cartman yelling "Kyle" is the definitive Kyle sound.
2. **"Kyle's Mom's a Bitch": South Park: Bigger, Longer & Uncut (1999). Film song.**
   It opens "Well, Kyle's mom's a bitch..." and is sung. Clip:
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NA1qpSKmteI. It is ~2 s, but "Kyle" alone is short. It is very funny
   at a party, but rude about the player's mum. Opt in only.
3. **The Jeremy Kyle Show (ITV, 2005–2019). UK TV.** There's no single shouted "Kyle"; the
   memes are "Look at her" and the lie-detector reveal. It is a fallback only: the theme sting plus
   a TTS "Kyle, to the stage".

## James

1. **"Bond. James Bond.": Dr. No (1962). Film.** Sean Connery's first line as Bond is at
   the baccarat table, Le Cercle (~0h10m). It is said slowly, cool, and the name is perfectly clean.
   Clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0xDj3NRYTU8 (~0:50 in). It is ~2 s with the theme stinging
   under it. *Why it works:* it is the most famous name introduction in cinema, and British.
   Any Bond's version works (Craig, Casino Royale 2006).
2. **Any later Bond, e.g. Daniel Craig, Casino Royale (2006).** It is the same line, with
   modern audio quality. Find it on getyarn.io with "bond james bond".
3. **"Sit Down": James (band, 1989/1991). UK song.** The band is named James, but the name is not
   sung. Only use the iconic chorus as walk-up music. There is no great "JAMES!" shout meme. The
   "yelling James" marmot GIF has no single canonical audio.

## Beth

1. **"Beth": KISS (1976, from *Destroyer*). Song.** The opening line is "Beth, I hear you
   calling", and "Beth" is the very first sung word, almost isolated. Audio (UMG):
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWFc1yUy1lM; also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P5Mb86osDrE.
   It is ~0:00–0:03, and "Beth, I hear you calling" is ~2.5 s. *Why it works:* it is literally a song
   about calling Beth. It is the perfect summons lyric, a bit cheesy, and fun to sing along to.
2. **Rick and Morty (Adult Swim, 2013–). TV.** Jerry whines "Beth!" constantly, but no single
   shout is canonical (GIPHY hosts many: https://giphy.com/explore/rick-and-morty-beth). Use
   getyarn.io with "beth" to pick one. It is ~0.6 s.
3. Fallback: TTS "BETH!", or Little Women (1994): Jo crying "Beth...". That is too sad for a party,
   so skip it.

## Free / public-domain summary
- Only **Joshua** has a realistic public-domain route (the traditional spiritual, plus pre-1955
  78 rpm releases that are out of UK copyright, or a self-rendered public-domain MIDI). Check the
  rights on the exact file first.
- There are no free clips of the others' pop-culture moments. The legal fallback for every name is
  **friend-recorded or TTS voice lines**, e.g. a mate doing a Dumbledore or Garvey impression,
  optionally over a CC0 sting from Freesound (filter by licence = CC0). Impressions of a line are
  far safer than the original audio.
